Seat-diversity rule

One voice per sector × region.

The Council enforces diversity by construction. Each (sector × region) cell in the matrix holds at most one voting Member. Multiple applicants from the same cell are weighed against each other by the Nominations & Conduct Committee; non-selected applicants are invited to the Public-Sector Observer tier (where applicable) or to the next Founding recruitment window.

Until LOIs are signed and ratified, cells remain empty. This is deliberate: it is both transparent and a call-to-action.

How cells are defined

  • Sector: derived from the applicant's primary operational domain. Mixed-sector organizations nominate the cell they most strongly represent; the Nominations & Conduct Committee confirms.
  • Region: the applicant's operational regional centre of gravity, not just headquarters. A multinational enterprise with its operational centre in APAC counts as APAC, even if legally incorporated elsewhere.
  • Academic / research and Public-sector / sovereign issuers: deliberately broader cells; these sectors benefit from higher representation.

Public-Sector Observer tier and the matrix

Public-Sector Observers do not count against the seat-diversity rule. A sovereign Observer and a voting Member from the same (sector × region) cell may coexist. This makes the Observer tier a working on-ramp for sovereigns who may later convert to voting membership.

Selection when multiple applicants target the same cell

When two or more LOI signers target the same cell, the Nominations & Conduct Committee recommends one for the voting seat based on published criteria (operational scale, track record, complementary expertise, jurisdiction fit). Non-selected applicants are invited to:

  • Public-Sector Observer tier (if eligible), or
  • The next Founding recruitment window, or
  • An adjacent cell where their profile fits (by mutual agreement).
